**Cognitive debt** is when you do not understand a part of the system, but rather accept what the agent gives you. It's like technical debt: ignoring it for too long leads to trouble. Agents have limited context window size, yours is bigger.
**Cognitive surrender** in the unconcious decision to give up thinking and becoming a prompting machine. Too much detail to keep in mind, and not really understanding the previous choices made.
**Cognitive offloading**: you keep the what, but use the agents for the how.

Countermeasures:
1. The 100 lines PR rule. Require the work to be split into smaller pieces that can be reviewed in focus.
2. Learning: you should understand the conceptual change before going on to implement it. Use the agent to teach you about concepts and use dialogue to form an opinion before moving on to code.
3. Friction / desired difficulty: purposefully designing stop point in your process to not let the implementation come about too quickly. Ensures that you have time to think and not just let go.
4. Don't review when you're tired.
5. Maintain the artisan capabilities. If you can't write code without the agent, then you have surrended.

Co-operation instead of delegation: your knowledge sharpens the prompt, which sharpens the model's output (mutual amplificaiton).
